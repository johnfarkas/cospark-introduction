The choice

The first step

Participating in the commonwealth starts with the Member Reserve Program.

This is a structured program with defined benefits, funded by corporate revenue. Your reserve stays secure, and the terms are set before you start.

Step 1 of 5 · You

It begins with you.

Every part of the model begins with a member deciding to participate. The reserve, the capability, and the Boost Payment all follow from this.

Step 2 of 5 · Contribute

You make a contribution. CoSpark holds it securely.

Any amount, any time, from $10. The contribution goes into your own reserve and stays there. Every dollar also creates a Point, the record of your commitment.

Step 3 of 5 · Capability

Combined reserves create real capability.

Because members commit for sixty months, CoSpark can plan against that commitment. Reserves taken together create institutional capability: reserve-backed businesses that generate revenue. This is the step that answers "where does the new money come from?"

Step 4 of 5 · Boost Payment

That capability funds your Boost Payment.

Cash back from CoSpark-generated funds every time you contribute, funded by corporate revenue. It arrives typically within 24 to 48 hours and is triggered by every contribution, with no vesting and no waiting period.

Step 5 of 5 · Sixty months

Redeem at five years.

After 60 months, in addition to the Boost Payments you have received in response to your contributions, you receive your full reserve back plus a bonus 5% premium.

How these numbers are calculated

Boost Payment equals your contribution divided by 3.25. Effective annual return is the internal rate of return on the full 60-month cash flow expressed as an annual equivalent. It lands between 16.7% and 17.2% depending on the timing convention used for the final redemption, which is why we state it as approximately 17%, and it holds at any contribution amount. Boost Payments and the 5% premium are taxable income and a 1099 is issued annually; the return of your own reserve is not taxable. Savings comparison applies the rate you set, compounded monthly on the same deposits. It opens at 0.62%, the national average savings yield reported by Bankrate on August 28, 2026.

Figures illustrate published program terms and are not a promise of future results.

The hard questions

Ask them. We would.

An offer worth taking survives scrutiny. Here are the questions people ask us most, answered the way we would want them answered.

Where does the money for the Boost Payment actually come from?

From corporate revenue. Because members commit to sixty months, CoSpark can plan against that commitment and put reserve-backed capability to work in businesses that generate income. A share of that income funds the Boost Payment.

What it is not: your Boost is funded by company revenue, and the reserves themselves stay intact and yours. A structure that pays early members out of later members' deposits is a different thing entirely, and it has a name.

Is this a multi-level marketing scheme?

No. Your Boost Payment is triggered by your own contribution and nothing else. Recruiting another member changes your returns not at all. There is no downline, no commission, no rank, and no volume requirement anywhere in the program.

Members do talk about CoSpark, because people who find something useful tend to. The economics of your reserve are settled entirely between you and the corporation.

What happens to my money if CoSpark fails?

This is the right question, and it deserves a candid answer. CoSpark is a C-corporation, and the Member Reserve Program is a contractual obligation of the company. It carries no FDIC or NCUA insurance, because CoSpark is a company rather than a bank or a credit union.

Your reserve is held as an obligation the company owes you. Like any obligation of any company, it depends on the company remaining sound. That is a material risk, and any member should weigh it. Ask to see the terms in writing, and ask your Advisory Board Member for the current financials before you commit a dollar.

How is a return near 17% possible when my bank pays under 1%?

Two reasons, and both are arithmetic rather than alchemy. First, the Boost Payment arrives within days of each contribution instead of at the end, so that money is in your hands earning or spending for the whole stretch. Time value does a great deal of the work.

Second, a bank pays you a sliver of what it earns lending your deposits out. CoSpark returns a share of what member commitment makes possible. Same underlying idea, a different split.

The national average savings account pays about 0.62% today, and that is where the calculator opens. Shop around and you can find high-yield accounts near 4%. Drag the rate up to 4% and compare against that instead — the Member Reserve still comes out ahead, by less.

What if I need to stop before sixty months?

You receive your contributions back, less the Boost Payments already paid to you, and the 5% completion premium stays with the program. Counting the Boosts you already received, you come out even on what you put in. The slider in the section above will show you the exact figures for any month.

Is this an investment?

The Member Reserve Program is a defined-benefit program funded by corporate revenue. It carries no market exposure, no fund manager, and no share of any security. The terms are set in writing before you contribute and they hold for the full sixty months.

Boost Payments and the 5% premium count as taxable income and you receive a 1099 each year. The return of your own reserve is not taxable.
